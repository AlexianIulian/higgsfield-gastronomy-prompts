# Registers — Haute Cuisine

**Register:** French · Michelin · precision · restraint  
**Models tested:** FLUX.2 Pro · soul_2  
**References:** Alain Ducasse, Joël Robuchon, Yannick Alléno, Le Bernardin

---

## The haute cuisine argument

Fine dining plating communicates through what it doesn't show as much as what it does. The wide plate margin is the argument: this kitchen has the confidence to serve you 40g of food on a 28cm plate and charge you €90 for the privilege. The photography has to make that argument visible.

**What the photography must show:**
1. The precision of every element's position
2. The relationship between protein, sauce, and garnish
3. The quality of the plate negative space (it's not empty — it's composed)
4. The surface texture of the proteins (crust, sear, poach quality)

---

## Prompt 01 — Poulet de Bresse / classic French

```
Single portion of roasted Poulet de Bresse breast on white Limoges plate,
skin golden-caramelised with precise crosshatch sear pattern,
dark jus pooled at 6 o'clock position, sauce stops 3cm from plate edge,
single chanterelle mushroom at 10 o'clock, micro-chervil at 11 o'clock,
camera at 45° three-quarter angle, warm 3600K overhead diffuse light,
specular highlight on jus surface, depth visible in sauce,
photorealistic haute cuisine photography, Alain Ducasse au Plaza Athénée aesthetic,
shot on Hasselblad H6D 100MP, 120mm, ultra high resolution,
tack sharp on crosshatch sear texture, medium DOF — full plate in focus,
white plate margin preserved — nothing above 12 o'clock position,
no second protein, no additional sauce elements
```

**`crosshatch sear pattern`** — specific detail that signals technical precision in the kitchen  
**`sauce stops 3cm from plate edge`** — the geometry of restraint. The sauce knows its place.  
**`nothing above 12 o'clock position`** — the top two-thirds of the plate is negative space

## Prompt 02 — Turbot / contemporary French

Seafood requires cooler temperature and different sauce behaviour.

```
Turbot fillet, skin-side up, slight golden crust visible,
placed at 7 o'clock on deep wide white plate,
beurre blanc emulsion pooled from 5 to 9 o'clock, slightly beneath fillet,
three thin asparagus tips at 11 o'clock, positioned parallel,
caviar at 12 o'clock — three pearls in line,
camera at 50° angle, overhead-leaning, soft diffuse 4000K,
no specular on emulsion surface — matte white sauce,
photorealistic fine dining photography, Le Bernardin aesthetic,
shot on Phase One IQ4, 90mm, ultra high resolution,
tack sharp on fish skin texture and caviar pearls,
cool to neutral palette — fish should not read warm,
plate margin generous — 40% of plate occupied
```

**`no specular on emulsion — matte white sauce`** — beurre blanc reflects diffusely, not speculately  
**`cool to neutral palette`** — fish reads wrong at warm temperature  
**`three pearls in line`** — specific garnish with specific geometry, not vague

## Prompt 03 — Dessert / pastry architecture

Desserts have different rules — height is part of the composition.

```
Soufflé au chocolat in individual copper cocotte, risen above rim by 3cm,
on white plate with fine bitter chocolate sauce poured tableside,
chocolate sauce in mid-pour, frozen at height of 15cm above plate,
camera at 30° low angle showing height of soufflé,
warm 3200K single source from left, shadow right — reveals height through shadow,
photorealistic pastry photography, Ducasse Alain pastry aesthetic,
shot on Canon EOS R5 85mm, ultra high resolution, tack sharp on soufflé surface texture,
chocolate sauce in sharp focus at top of pour, plate and cocotte slightly soft,
warm amber palette — chocolate richness preserved
```

**Low camera angle** — the only register where horizontal/low reads as haute cuisine: showing the height of something risen.  
**`sauce in mid-pour, frozen at height of 15cm`** — action photography applied to gastronomy. The moment of tableside service.
